# Lower bounds for the diagonal Gallai–van der Waerden numbers: GW(r; 4, 4) ≥ 329 for r = 4, 5, 6

A Lean 4 proof, with Mathlib, that the Gallai–van der Waerden numbers `GW(r; 4, 4)` of Mao, Ozeki,
Robertson and Wang are at least 329 for four, five and six colours, and that the canonical number
`H(4)` is at least 329. It is laid out for registration on [Palomar](https://palomar-registry.org):
`Challenge.lean` states the results, `Solution.lean` proves them, and `comparator.json` names the
compared theorems.

## The result

Write `[1, n] = {1, …, n}`. An exact `r`-colouring of `[1, n]` uses all `r` colours. A `k`-term
arithmetic progression (AP) is rainbow when its terms have pairwise distinct colours, and an
`l`-term AP is monochromatic when its terms all have one colour; common differences are at least 1.
Following Mao, Ozeki, Robertson and Wang (MORW; J. Combin. Theory Ser. A 193 (2023) 105672,
Definition 1.15), `GW(r; k, l)` is the least `M` such that for every `n ≥ M`, every exact
`r`-colouring of `[1, n]` contains a rainbow `k`-term AP or a monochromatic `l`-term AP.

**Theorem.** For `r = 4, 5, 6`, the interval `[1, 328]` has an exact `r`-colouring with no rainbow
4-term AP and no monochromatic 4-term AP. Hence `GW(r; 4, 4) ≥ 329 = 3 · 109 + 2` for `r = 4, 5, 6`,
and `H(4) ≥ 329`, where `H(4)` is the least `n` such that every colouring of `[1, n]`, with any
number of colours, has a rainbow or a monochromatic 4-term AP.

For comparison, `GW(3; 4, 4) = w(3; 4) = 293` (MORW, Proposition 4.1, and Kouril's van der
Waerden number), and the previous published lower bound for `GW(4; 4, 4)` was 186 (Y. Si,
arXiv:2610.30711).

## What is compared (`comparator.json`)

All four theorems are stated in `Challenge.lean` (with `sorry`) and proved in `Solution.lean`.

| Lean name | What it says |
|---|---|
| `Gw444.exists_good` | For `4 ≤ r ≤ 6` there is `c : ℕ → Fin r` with `IsGoodExactColouring r 4 4 328 c`. |
| `Gw444.lt_of_gwProperty` | For `4 ≤ r ≤ 6`, every `M` with `GWProperty r 4 4 M` satisfies `328 < M`. |
| `Gw444.lt_GW` | For `4 ≤ r ≤ 6`, if some `M` has `GWProperty r 4 4 M`, then `328 < GW r 4 4`. |
| `Gw444.exists_canonical` | Some `c : ℕ → ℕ` has no rainbow 4-term AP and no monochromatic 4-term AP in `[1, 328]`. |

`definition_names` is empty on purpose. The three definitions the statements use are spelled out in
full in `Challenge.lean`; Comparator follows them from the theorem statements and requires them to be
identical in the Solution, which takes them from `Gw444/Defs.lean`.

## Definitions, and how they match the paper

The definitions are those of the registered formalization
[PALOMAR-2026-11-12-000009](https://palomar-registry.org/entry.html?id=PALOMAR-2026-11-12-000009)
(Gallai–van der Waerden numbers from `m`-th power residue colourings), with the namespace renamed:

- `IsGoodExactColouring r k l n c`: every colour `col : Fin r` occurs on `[1, n]`; no
  `x, x + d, …, x + (k - 1)d` in `[1, n]` with `d ≥ 1` has pairwise distinct colours; no
  `x, x + d, …, x + (l - 1)d` in `[1, n]` with `d ≥ 1` is monochromatic. The colouring
  `c : ℕ → Fin r` is defined on all of `ℕ`; only its values on `[1, n]` matter.
- `GWProperty r k l M`: for every `n ≥ M` and every `c`, `¬ IsGoodExactColouring r k l n c`. This is
  the defining property in MORW Definition 1.15.
- `GW r k l := sInf {M | GWProperty r k l M}`. In Lean the infimum of the empty set of natural
  numbers is `0`. That the set is not empty is the Gallai–van der Waerden theorem, which is not
  formalized here; so `lt_GW` assumes that some threshold exists, while `lt_of_gwProperty` (every
  threshold exceeds 328) needs no assumption.

`H(4)` is not defined in Lean; `exists_canonical` states its lower bound directly.

## How the proof goes

**The colourings** (`Gw444/Colourings.lean`, generated by `scripts/make_colourings.py`). Let
`p = 109`, a prime with `p ≡ 1 (mod 3)`. A point `X ∈ [1, 328]` with `X ≢ 1 (mod 109)` gets colour
0, 1 or 2 according to the cubic residue class of `X - 1` modulo 109 (the value of
`(X - 1)^36 mod 109`: `1`, `45` or `63`). The four points `1, 110, 219, 328` get the colours
`0, 3, 3, 0` (`r = 4`), `3, 4, 3, 4` (`r = 5`) and `3, 4, 5, 3` (`r = 6`). The 4-colouring is the
one in the file `gw/colouring_1_328.txt` of the public data repository (up to the names of the
colours; the script checks this). Each colouring is stored as a natural number in base 8.

**The check** (`Gw444/Check.lean`). The colour of a point is read from the base-8 number by the
kernel's built-in `Nat` operations (`cv`). Structurally recursive Boolean tests check that every
colour occurs on `[1, n]` (`allCols`) and, for each difference `d`, that every 4-term progression
`x, x + d, x + 2d, x + 3d` in `[1, 328]` has two terms of one colour and a term of another colour
than its first (`rowTest`, over a range of differences `rangeTest`). `good_of_tests` proves that
these tests imply `IsGoodExactColouring`. For each colouring Lean's kernel evaluates `allCols` and
`rangeTest` on seven pieces of the range of differences `1 ≤ d ≤ 109`, each in its own declaration,
by `decide +kernel` (a few seconds in all).

**The bounds** (`Solution.lean`). A good exact `r`-colouring of `[1, 328]` shows that no `M ≤ 328`
has the defining property (`lt_of_good`), hence `328 < GW r 4 4` when some threshold exists
(`lt_GW_of_good`). The 4-colouring, read as a colouring by natural numbers, gives
`exists_canonical`.

The cubic-residue construction behind the colourings for `r = 5, 6` is the case `m = 3`, `p = 109`
of Theorem 8 of PALOMAR-2026-11-12-000009. The 4-colouring (Proposition 3.5 of the paper cited
below: the zero class gets the class of the cubes at its two ends) is not an instance of that entry,
which gives `GW(4; 4, 4) > 327` only. Here the general theorems
are not used: each colouring is checked directly.

No `sorry` outside `Challenge.lean`, no `native_decide`, no extra axioms:
`lake env lean scripts/CheckAxioms.lean` prints `[propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound]` (or a
subset) for each compared theorem.

## Why it is of interest

Gallai–van der Waerden numbers were introduced by Mao, Ozeki, Robertson and Wang (2023) as the
arithmetic analogue of Gallai–Ramsey numbers. The diagonal value `GW(4; 4, 4)` is the first open
value of the diagonal. Quantavane's paper reduces it to a single instance (`GW(4; 4, 4) = 329` if and
only if `[1, 329]` has no good exact 4-colouring), which is not decided; the lower bound 329
formalized here is the best known. The canonical number `H(4)` is the diagonal canonical van der
Waerden number of Erdős and Graham, whose growth is Problem #190 of the Erdős problems database;
`H(4) ≥ 329` improves the standard bound `H(4) ≥ w(3; 4) = 293` that three colours give. The natural
readers are researchers in Ramsey theory on the integers and arithmetic combinatorics: van der
Waerden numbers, rainbow (anti-van der Waerden) problems and Gallai–Ramsey colourings.

## Provenance, literature and novelty

The bound and the colourings were found by Quantavane's computer search and residue constructions
on 11 and 12 November 2026 and were presented before this repository: on the results page of
quantavane.com ("GW(4;4,4) ≥ 329 from cubic residues modulo 109") and in the public data repository
[arhancanli/quantavane-gw-data](https://github.com/arhancanli/quantavane-gw-data) (file
`gw/colouring_1_328.txt`), which accompanies Quantavane's paper "Power-residue colourings and exact
Gallai–van der Waerden numbers" (A. Canli, November 2026, not yet published; Theorem 1.5(a) there).
This repository formalizes that result. A literature search on 11 and 12 November 2026 (MORW; Si's
arXiv paper; MathWorld; arXiv, Zenodo, GitHub, OEIS, Crossref and the Palomar registry) found no
lower bound for `GW(4; 4, 4)` above Si's 186. The search was limited to these sources, so novelty is
our reading of it, not an established fact.

## Checking it

Lean `v4.35.0` (`lean-toolchain`) and Mathlib `v4.35.0`
(`56a005855ea17e0b0958f73ec801ae140b0a1679`, pinned in `lake-manifest.json`).

```sh
lake exe cache get        # prebuilt Mathlib
lake build                # Gw444, Challenge, Solution (the kernel checks take a few seconds)
lake env lean scripts/CheckAxioms.lean
lake comparator           # Linux with bubblewrap: compares Challenge and Solution and replays the proofs
python3 scripts/make_colourings.py Gw444/Colourings.lean   # regenerates the colourings
```

`.github/workflows/palomar-preflight.yml` runs Palomar's own mechanical check
(`PalomarRegistry/PalomarSubmission`, pinned commit) on every push to `main`.
